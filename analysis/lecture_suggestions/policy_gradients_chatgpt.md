**Board derivation for slides 6–10.** Set \(J(\boldsymbol\theta)=\mathbb E_{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}[G_0]\). In a finite discrete example, differentiate each path probability times its recorded return. Its probability derivative equals its probability times its log-probability derivative. With policy-independent initial distribution and dynamics, only the log-policy terms survive. The full-return expression and the reward-to-go expression are

\[
\nabla J(\boldsymbol\theta)
=\mathbb E_{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}\!\left[ G_0\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\log\pi(A_t\mid S_t,\boldsymbol\theta)\right]
=\mathbb E_{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t G_t\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\log\pi(A_t\mid S_t,\boldsymbol\theta)\right].
\]

In the second expression, conditioning on the state and action replaces \(G_t\) by \(q^{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(S_t,A_t)\). That gives the theorem in the same episodic, time-summed convention, including the effect of the policy on visited states. No second proof or new visitation-distribution symbol is needed. The two sample sums can differ on a particular episode. The equality is between expectations. For teaching notes, the differentiation uses a differentiable policy with fixed support; the Gaussian example illustrates sampling a density, while the board proof uses discrete paths.

### Worked case and answer key

Two decisions, then termination. At the start, right takes the agent to the upper branch and left takes it to the lower branch. Either move earns 2. At the upper branch the final rewards for right/left are 4/2; at the lower branch they are 2/0. Set γ = ½. Use the same binary policy preference at all three states, initially zero, so both actions have probability ½. The example is local to this deck.

- **One episode.** Start → right → upper → left → terminal. Rewards are 2 and 2. Returns are 3 and 2. Scores for the adjustable preference are +½ and −½. The reward-to-go sum is \(3(½)+(½)2(-½)=1\). With α = 0.1 the preference increases from 0 to 0.1. The full-return sum on this episode is \(3(½-½)=0\). This makes the “equal in expectation” point visible.
- **Variance at the upper state.** Compare the local weight times score, before multiplying by the common outer discount. The two rows are equally likely. Without a baseline their mean is 0.5 and variance is 2.25. Centering at the state value 3 leaves the mean at 0.5 and makes the variance zero in this example. With baseline 10 the two contributions are −3 and 4, with variance 12.25. These are conditional variances for one gradient component. Whole-episode gradients and learning curves have other sources of variation.

| Upper-state action | Return | Score | Return × score | (Return − 3) × score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Right | 4 | +½ | 2 | ½ |
| Left | 2 | −½ | −1 | ½ |

- **Baseline identity.** Conditional on \(S_t=s\), the expected score is \(\sum_a\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol\theta)\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\log\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol\theta)=\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\sum_a\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol\theta)=0\). Multiplying by any fixed state baseline preserves zero. For a learned baseline, form actor weights from a predictor fixed before those actions were sampled and stop gradients through its predictions in the actor step. Fitting that predictor on prior rollouts gives a clean classroom implementation.
- **Advantage.** Upper and lower state values are 3 and 1. At the start, the action values for right/left are 3.5/2.5 and the state value is 3. Right's true advantage is +0.5. In our sample its centered return is 3 − 3 = 0. A student who labels that single zero the true advantage has confused a sample with its conditional mean.
- **Where critic error enters.** Keep the start prediction at 3 but give the upper and lower states predictions of 0 and 1. Then the first-step bootstrap targets for right/left are 2 and 2.5, reversing the correct ranking. Centering both by the same start prediction preserves that reversal. Under MC, a wrong state-only baseline still cancels in the expected score. Here the successor error occurs preferentially after choosing right. This isolates the source of actor bias without spending Week 8's worked-update time.
