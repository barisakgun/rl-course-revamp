## Consequential checks and recommendations

1. **Keep at least 21 days after prerequisite-ready release.** A1 is the exception to watch: an October 29 release followed by November 16 submission is only 18 days; October 26 to November 16 is 21. Early Week 7 works only if teaching/makeups enable early Week 4 release. Otherwise move A1's deadline within Week 7 rather than releasing untaught assessed material or silently shortening its window. Apply the same rule after actual dates are assigned to A2–A4.
2. **Keep calendar dates tied to coverage.** MT1 needs the foundation/approximation material through logical 5.2; MT2 needs policy methods through logical 9.1; MT3 needs offline RL through logical 12.2. Their calendar-week labels do not establish that those lessons occurred. October and November makeup placements therefore affect exam readiness, even though the topic sequence and minutes stay fixed. Leave preparation time after the final assessed lesson rather than placing a makeup immediately before an exam.
3. **Use early Week 13 for MT3.** December 31, 2026 is Thursday; interpret the instructor's “January 31 / New Year's Eve” as December 31. December 28–30 is the workable part of the proposed exam week given the stated year-end concern and January absence. This is a date-range recommendation, not a booking or a verified university holiday claim.
4. **Keep the early/late separation in Weeks 7 and 11.** Aim for several days between deliverables, for example Monday/Friday, once releases permit. Week 11 progress provides a useful checkpoint before the presentation and final report; do not expand its scope because it moved a week later. Prefer A4 early enough in Week 15 to leave roughly a week before the Week 16 final report, conditional on its 21-day window.
5. **Week 14 presentations are workable if TA availability is secured.** Retaining three two-hour slots of eight teams yields 104 minutes of talks/discussion and 16 minutes for transitions per slot. Keep students attending only their own slot. A brief peer form can be completed during attendance; avoid a separate peer-review essay. Recommend a common rubric, no self-grading and TA moderation of peer marks; the TA/peer weighting remains a decision for later. Recordings enable instructor review but do not eliminate staff time: six hours to host, with up to six additional hours if the instructor later watches everything.
6. **Confirm the final grading interval.** Week 16 aligns with the stated third-week-of-January final-report target. Its consistency with “one week before letter grades” still depends on the actual grade deadline and who can assess reports/recordings during January. Do not infer that absence from live teaching prevents asynchronous grading, or that such grading is already covered.
