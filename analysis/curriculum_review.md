## IQL and the optimism/pessimism connection

The contrast should focus on the ability to obtain corrective evidence. An online agent can choose an uncertain action and learn what happens; a fixed-data learner cannot resolve unsupported-action errors through fresh interaction. This motivates exploration bonuses in appropriate online settings and constraints/conservative treatment of unsupported actions offline. It does not imply that every online method is optimistic, or that online value overestimation is desirable.

IQL needs a precise place in this picture. It can favor higher values **within dataset support** through asymmetric/expectile fitting while avoiding explicit maximization over unseen actions during critic fitting; it then extracts a policy through advantage-weighted fitting. It should not be described as merely a pessimistic lower-bound method. CQL provides the short contrast of explicitly penalizing unsupported values. [CS224R offline slides, pp. 24–26](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/slides/07_cs224r_offline_rl_2026.pdf#page=24), [CS285 CQL discussion, pp. 18–21](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/static/slides/lec-18.pdf#page=18).

The candidate gives Week 12 **110 teaching minutes**, leaving nine minutes beyond the generic buffer, by calibrating BC to high-level motivation. This fits conceptual IQL with a small numerical example, but remains low-confidence if the goal expands to full implementation readiness. Shared examples reduce switching overhead; they do not eliminate the new expectile/weighted-policy prerequisites. Either a five-minute LSTD or IRL mention can use that margin; adding both would consume generic buffer or require a further displacement. Both remain explicit optional notes, not base content.

LSTD is useful historical context for learning a value estimator from batches, but it solves an empirical TD fixed-point system rather than ordinary supervised regression against observed value labels. BC remains the cleaner supervised-policy bridge; IQL's weighted policy extraction extends that bridge. [Sutton–Barto §9.8, printed pp. 228–229](../sources/RLbook2020.pdf#page=250). Average reward is a different continuing-task objective, not simply setting the discount factor to one in the existing return equations. [§10.3, printed p. 249](../sources/RLbook2020.pdf#page=271).
