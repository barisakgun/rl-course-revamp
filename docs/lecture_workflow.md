# Lecture-deck workflow (Phase 7, pilot v2)

**Shared** by the instructor, Claude and ChatGPT. Pilot v2 was adopted by the instructor on 2026-10-09 after the
session 1.1 and 1.2 pilots; see `decisions/decision_log.md`. It replaces the 2026-10-06 workflow, which remains in git
history. Inputs:
- `analysis/phase7_workflow_review_claude.md`;
- `analysis/phase7_workflow_review_chatgpt.md`;
- the instructor's comments on both.

Its main changes from the pilot:
- go to the PPTX early and iterate on it;
- no polishing of text versions or of decks the instructor has not seen;
- the PPTX is the maintained source from the first render;
- AI content and error checks after each round of instructor edits;
- a full PowerPoint render review only at the end.

`AGENTS.md` / `CLAUDE.md` (shared lecture-content conventions, file ownership) and `docs/repository_contract.md` still
apply. This file fixes the order of steps and where each artifact lives.

## Files per deck

`<deck>` is a stable snake-case deck name (e.g. `introduction`); `weekNN` is the week of the deck's first session.

| Artifact | Path | Owner | Maintained? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial suggestions | `analysis/lecture_suggestions/<deck>_{claude,chatgpt}.md` | each AI | Historical after step 3 |
| Reviews and responses | `analysis/lecture_suggestions/<deck>_review.md` | shared | Yes: one file for both AIs' findings, responses and resolutions |
| **Deck decisions** | `decisions/lectures/<deck>.md` | shared | **Yes: accepted and open content decisions, and the deck's only current status** |
| First-render content | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_content.md` | shared | No: input to the first render, frozen at that render |
| First-render renderer input | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude.yaml` | Claude | No: first draft, frozen at the first render; maintained only if the instructor asks |
| Deck | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude.pptx` | Claude renders; instructor edits | The teaching source from the first render on |
| Review views | text view `output/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude.md`; previews `output/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude_preview.{pdf,png}` | generated by Claude | Text view regenerated per saved deck; previews at the final check (quick previews during iteration are not stored) |

**Status in one place.** The deck decision file holds the deck's current status and fingerprint. Other artifacts point
to it instead of restating it: the review, the content-file header and the YAML header. The decision log gets one line
per batch of deck decisions and one at "done". Historical reviews keep the versions they examined.

**Frozen first-render inputs.** At the first render, mark the content file and the YAML as historical. Their headers
point to the deck decision file. The YAML header reads: "First draft: renderer input for the first render of this deck
only (date). Not maintained: the PPTX is the source from that render on. Do not rebuild over the deck or treat this file
as its current content." A PPTX cannot always be converted back to YAML: equation-slide bodies and PowerPoint-only
content are not recoverable. So the draft is kept rather than regenerated.

**Versions.** Decks are git-ignored. This semester, Dropbox history and `output/deck_backups/` keep deck versions;
Claude backs up a deck before every change it makes.

**Reviews.** Keep the shared review lightweight. Each finding has a stable ID, author and date, the affected slide, the
issue and its consequence, a proposed fix, responses and a status (`open`, `resolved` or `deferred`). Label it as an
**error**, a **teaching risk**, an **optional preference** or **maintenance**. Identify the content revision or saved
deck under review; exported views must match it. Read a slide in context before reporting a missing explanation.
Resolving a finding does not accept a curriculum decision. Agreement between the AIs is not acceptance: a decision stays
open until the instructor decides or explicitly delegates. The deck decision file contains only outcomes, each one
either **accepted** (explicit instructor decision, with its date) or **open** (with a pointer to the discussion).

## Steps

1. **Initial suggestions (both AIs, independently).** Use the accepted session scope, mastery, worked activity and
   minutes (`decisions/topic_decisions.yaml`) and `output/lectures/session_sources.md`.
   - **Pull what is relevant from all sources:** the existing course (`sources/current_course/`), the book, the
     configured reference courses (local slides in `sources/<id>/lectures/`), with provenance. They are evidence, not
     authority.
   - **Delta against the previous course where its slides are substantial.** A session may draw on several old decks,
     and an old deck may span several sessions. Give a compact keep / cut / fix / move comparison with deck and slide
     references, grouping related slides where that is clearer. If unsure whether the existing material is substantial,
     ask the instructor. Routine source selection needs no permission. No slide-by-slide comparison against every
     external course and no separate source audit.
   - **Earlier redesigned decks are context, not the base:** they give continuity (notation, running examples, what
     students already saw). They do not replace the sources for the new session.
   - **Introductory context and motivation.** Assume students meet the ideas for the first time. For each major new
     concept or quantity, make clear:
     - the problem it addresses;
     - its meaning and purpose, before or alongside the formal definition;
     - its connection to what students know;
     - an example, interpretation or question that checks understanding.

     These criteria apply to the teaching sequence, not as a per-slide template. When material is proposed for removal
     or compression, account for its teaching purpose: keep it elsewhere, explain why it is unnecessary, or name its
     accepted later placement.
   - **Unpolished:** a concise slide-level teaching sequence with reasons, alternatives and approximate timing impact.
     Wording and visuals can wait. Correctness and the central "why" cannot.
2. **One mutual cross-review**, in the shared review file. Check:
   - mathematics and source use;
   - context, motivation and sequencing;
   - accepted commitments;
   - approximate feasibility;
   - assumptions both suggestions share, such as knowing why a quantity is useful before the lecture has said so.

   Resolve straightforward corrections; present only consequential choices to the instructor. No repeated prose
   refinement.
3. **Instructor choices**, recorded once in `decisions/lectures/<deck>.md`. Use the AI the instructor names, or
   otherwise the first to act; the other checks the record and does not add a second one.
4. **First-render content (ChatGPT).** ChatGPT consolidates the choices into `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_content.md`,
   marked shared, containing:
   - stable slide IDs where practical and the order;
   - exact reuse references and edit lists ("old deck X, PPTX slide N, with edits Y");
   - the necessary new displayed content;
   - correct equations (old decks' PowerPoint equations first, LaTeX where needed);
   - essential speaker cues and answers.

   It must not contain incorrect equations, factual placeholders or missing essential explanations. There is no separate
   review of this text proposal: Claude raises substantive issues found while rendering in the shared review.
5. **First render and handoff (Claude).** Claude renders an editable PPTX, copying old slides natively (equations and
   diagrams kept), with a quick LibreOffice preview for gross problems. At most one correctness-only AI pass before
   handoff (ChatGPT); no polishing of a deck the instructor has not seen. Freeze the content file and the YAML as
   described above. From here the saved PPTX is the source. Never rebuild over an edited deck.
6. **Iteration: instructor edits ↔ AI content and error check**, repeated as needed. After each saved round, Claude
   regenerates the text view and identifies the version. Both AIs check what changed and what it affects; a model or
   notation change can affect distant equations and notes. They check:
   - facts, mathematics, notation (against `course/notation_guide.md`) and consistency;
   - context, motivation, sequence and likely student misconceptions;
   - **speaker notes:** agreement with the slide, answers, cues, stale references;
   - approximate timing and protection of worked activities;
   - visual changes a text diff cannot show: diagrams, equation formatting, images, layout, slide visibility.

   For the visual checks, use quick previews: LibreOffice (Claude) or ChatGPT/Codex's own tool. Where these cannot show
   a feature faithfully, render the affected slides in PowerPoint from the system temp folder. Mark stale previews, and
   do not treat an unreliable preview as proof of a defect or of correctness. Findings go to the shared review. Claude
   applies the fixes the instructor approves directly in the PPTX, backs up first, and refreshes the affected views. The
   reviewer then rechecks the affected material only.
7. **Final complete check.** Run this when substantive iteration settles.
   - Both AIs do a full content and notes review.
   - Claude inspects a fresh PowerPoint render of every slide, including hidden slides and their intended status.
   - If equations were edited outside PowerPoint, Claude first refreshes their fallback pictures
     (`scripts/refresh_math_fallbacks_claude.py`).
   - Animations, builds, media and links are checked from the file's definitions. Where only playback can confirm them,
     the instructor checks in slideshow mode.
   - Fix remaining issues and recheck what the fixes affect.

   Exported previews and the text view must correspond to the identified PPTX.
8. **Done.** On the instructor's acceptance, the deck decision file records "done" with the deck's SHA-256, and the
   decision log gets one line. Generated previews and the text view come from the accepted PPTX. A later substantive
   change reopens the deck's status. Acceptance is not inherited.

When an instructor edit changes scope, a prerequisite, a running example or another cross-session commitment, reconcile
it with the decision records. Wording and layout edits need no log entries.

## Timing convention

Plan and time every deck as if delivery goes to plan: use the session's accepted teaching minutes from
`decisions/topic_decisions.yaml`. Time lost in class (an overrun, extra questions or an early finish) is not carried into
later decks' calculations, and unfinished slides are not re-timed into the next session. Each lecture's configured buffer
absorbs it (instructor, 2026-10-07). Slide targets in the notes are planning estimates. Arithmetic and consistency checks
stay exact, but report them as "targets sum to X", not as proof that the lecture fits. Protect student calculation,
explanation and discussion time; required spoken content counts even when it is only in the notes. Changes to accepted
allocations or ceilings go through the normal decision process.

## Important decisions (reasons to pause)

Pause and ask the instructor only for:
- a change to accepted scope, mastery, exclusions or minutes;
- a running-example or other cross-session choice;
- notation not in the guide, or a guide ambiguity the material hits;
- an unresolved disagreement between the AIs about content or old-slide reuse;
- a correctness doubt that cannot be settled from the sources;
- uncertainty about whether old material for the session is substantial (step 1).

Everything else proceeds and is listed for the instructor at the next natural stop.

## Evaluating pilot v2

After the next deck, add a short retrospective to its shared review. Cover:
- whether the first PPTX kept the relevant source explanations;
- whether the instructor had to restore missing motivation;
- which consequential errors the checks caught;
- where repeated decisions or synchronization work cost effort.

How much the instructor edits is not a failure measure: editing is part of preparing the lecture.
