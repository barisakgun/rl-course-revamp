# Lecture-deck workflow (Phase 7)

**Shared** by the instructor, Claude and ChatGPT. Adopted by the instructor on 2026-10-06, during the session 1.1 pilot; see `decisions/decision_log.md`. It merges three inputs:
- the instructor's proposed sequence;
- ChatGPT's Q12 recommendation and refinement (`analysis/phase7_brainstorming.md`);
- Claude's round-2 adjustments (`analysis/lecture_suggestions/introduction_round2_claude.md`).

The instructor added the per-deck decision file. Updated on 2026-10-06 to keep one shared review file and one editable content file through the AI review-and-fix loop, until the instructor takes over the PPTX. The workflow is evaluated after the 1.1 pilot and may be shortened by explicit instructor instruction.

`AGENTS.md` (shared lecture-content conventions, file ownership) and `docs/repository_contract.md` still apply. This file only fixes the order of steps and where each artifact lives.

## Files per deck

`<deck>` is a stable snake-case deck name (e.g. `introduction`); `weekNN` is the week of the deck's first session.

| Artifact | Path | Owner | Maintained? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial suggestions | `analysis/lecture_suggestions/<deck>_{claude,chatgpt}.md` | each AI | Historical after step 3 |
| Reviews and responses | `analysis/lecture_suggestions/<deck>_review.md` | shared | Yes: one file for both AIs' findings, responses and resolutions |
| **Deck decisions** | `decisions/lectures/<deck>.md` | shared | **Yes: the single list of accepted and open content decisions for the deck** |
| Render-ready content | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_content.md` | shared | Yes through proposal/deck review and fixes; frozen when the instructor takes over editing |
| Deck source (renderer input) | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude.yaml` | Claude | Content follows the shared Markdown until instructor takeover; afterward kept in step with the edited PPTX |
| Deck | `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_claude.pptx` | Claude renders; instructor edits | Rendered from the content proposal during AI iteration; teaching authority when the instructor takes over |
| Review views | `output/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_preview.pdf` / `.png` and a text view | generated by Claude | Regenerated per saved deck |

Mark the content and review files as **shared** in their headers. Keep existing AI-specific reviews and round files as historical references; do not rename or rewrite them. From consolidation onward, carry any still-relevant open findings into the shared review with links to their origin, and append subsequent exchanges there rather than creating new round files. The initial independent suggestions remain separate.

Keep the shared review lightweight: give findings stable IDs, author/date, the affected slide or content section, a proposed fix, responses and a status (`open`, `resolved` or `deferred`). Identify the content revision or saved deck being reviewed; exported previews/text must match that deck. Preserve the reasoning when marking an item resolved. Record short change summaries in this review file, not as a second discussion thread in the content file. Resolving a review finding does not itself accept a curriculum decision.

Reviews contain discussion; the deck decision file contains only outcomes. Each entry in the decision file is one of:
- **accepted:** explicit instructor decision, with its date;
- **open:** with a pointer to where it is discussed and, if useful, both AIs' recommendations.

Agreement between the AIs is not acceptance. It stays "open" until the instructor decides or explicitly delegates. The decision log gets one line per batch of deck decisions, pointing to the deck file; it does not repeat the list.

## Steps

1. **Initial suggestions.** Claude and ChatGPT each write one independently, following the compact shape in brainstorming Q8.
   - Inputs: the accepted lecture plan (`decisions/topic_decisions.yaml`), `output/lectures/session_sources.md`, the notation guide, the instructor's old decks, the book and the configured reference courses.
   - Local copies of the reference-course slides are in `sources/<id>/lectures/`; refresh them with `python3 scripts/download_source_slides.py`.
   - At least the reference courses' slides for the same sessions are checked. They are evidence, not authority.
2. **Mutual cross-review**, at least one iteration. Each AI reads the other's initial suggestion read-only and records findings and proposed fixes in the shared review file. It then responds to the other's findings in that same file, identifying resolved items and remaining disputes. Existing pilot reviews remain historical as described above.
3. **Instructor comments.** One AI records the outcome once in `decisions/lectures/<deck>.md`: the AI the instructor names, or otherwise the first to act on the comments. The other AI checks the record and does not add a second one.
4. **Render-ready content (ChatGPT).** ChatGPT writes `course/lectures/weekNN/<deck>_content.md`, marked shared in its header. It consolidates the accepted choices and agreed fixes rather than concatenating the two suggestions. It contains:
   - stable slide IDs and the intended displayed content;
   - exact reuse references ("old deck X, PPTX slide N, with edits Y");
   - equations: reuse from old decks first, LaTeX only where needed;
   - only the teaching notes or reveal cues that are needed. A blank discussion slide gets a short note; the instructor's live reveal is not scripted.

   Reuse the same content file throughout iteration; do not create per-AI or per-round alternatives. It is the maintained teaching-content proposal until the instructor takes over the PPTX. ChatGPT pauses for the instructor only on an important decision (see below).
5. **Proposal review (Claude).** Claude records findings and responses in the shared review, makes agreed corrections directly in the shared content file, and records a short change summary in the review. Substantive alternatives go to the instructor. Repeat review and revision in these same files as needed. Decisions taken here go into the deck decision file.
6. **Rendering (Claude).** Claude generates its deck YAML from the shared content file and renders the PPTX. Old slides are copied through PowerPoint, so their native equations and diagrams are kept. Claude runs the usual technical checks and exports the review views (preview PDF/PNG and text view) for the saved deck. The content file remains editable. YAML may hold rendering/layout details, but does not become an independent teaching-content proposal during this loop.
7. **Deck review (ChatGPT).** ChatGPT reviews the previews and text view; read-only inspection of the PPTX is allowed for this review. It checks fidelity to the current content file and decisions, mathematics, notation, diagrams, notes and visible layout. Findings and responses go in the same shared review file.
8. **Fix pass (Claude).** Claude applies agreed findings before handoff to the instructor. Content changes go into the shared content file and then into the renderer input and PPTX; layout-only repairs belong in the rendering implementation. Refresh the review views after changes. Repeat steps 5–8 as needed, rechecking the affected material and resolving findings in the shared review. Important unresolved disagreements go to the instructor as a short list.
9. **Instructor takes over and edits** the PPTX directly. At this handoff, freeze the content file as the historical proposal, identify the handed-off deck version, and point to the PPTX/YAML as the subsequent maintained artifacts. The instructor-edited deck takes precedence. Do not keep editing the frozen Markdown proposal in parallel with the deck YAML.
10. **Pull-back (Claude).** Claude reconciles the edited PPTX into the deck YAML (`scripts/pull_deck_claude.py`) and regenerates the text view.
    - This is a semantic check: deleted slides, changed examples and intentional blank space are carried over.
    - Content that cannot be represented is reported, not invented.
    - Claude never rebuilds over an edited deck.
    - Decision-relevant edits, such as a changed example or a dropped topic, are noted in the deck decision file.
    - Any follow-up findings stay in the same shared review file; the frozen proposal is not reopened merely to mirror the edited deck.
11. **Done.** On the instructor's thumbs-up, the deck decision file records "done" with the deck's SHA-256, and the decision log gets one line. A later substantive change reopens the deck's status. Acceptance is not inherited.

## Timing convention

Plan and time every deck as if delivery goes to plan: use the session's accepted teaching minutes from `decisions/topic_decisions.yaml`. Time lost in class, such as an overrun, extra questions or an early finish, is not carried into later decks' calculations, and unfinished slides are not re-timed into the next session. Each lecture's configured buffer absorbs it. (Instructor, 2026-10-07.)

## Important decisions (reasons to pause)

Pause and ask the instructor only for:
- a change to accepted scope, mastery, exclusions or minutes;
- a running-example or other cross-session choice;
- notation not in the guide, or a guide ambiguity the material hits;
- an unresolved disagreement between the AIs about content or old-slide reuse;
- a correctness doubt that cannot be settled from the sources.

Everything else proceeds and is listed for the instructor at the next natural stop.
